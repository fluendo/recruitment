# Question 2: Extending Anonymization with Efficient Depth-Based Background Filtering

## 1. Depth model: run a small model directly

The task needs **relative** depth (ordering, not calibrated distance)
and only needs enough resolution to drive the masking mechanism below.
That relaxes the requirement enough that running a small pretrained
monocular depth model directly, per frame, is a reasonable default:

- **Depth-Anything-V2 "Small"** (~25M params, ViT-S backbone) is the
  light end of that model family and a natural first choice.

- CNN-based alternatives (**MiDaS-small**, **FastDepth**, and similar
  MobileNet/EfficientNet-backbone depth nets) are worth benchmarking on the
  actual target hardware, since convolutional models are often cheaper than
  ViT at comparable output quality. Only coarse, locally-consistent relative
  depth is needed, so the fastest model that gives clean-enough local
  boundaries is the right pick, not the newest or largest one. I would start
  with Depth-Anything-V2 Small as a model to beat, and switch to one of these 
  if it is too slow on the target hardware and perform similarly.

- For **fixed-camera, resource-constrained deployments with representative
  prior footage** (smart city, retail, workplace safety), the background is
  also stable from frame to frame. That allows two more optimizations that
  are not safe in the general case: refreshing the background depth only
  periodically instead of every frame, and/or distilling a small model
  specialized to that camera's scene. This only works when the scene really
  is stable and there is prior footage to specialize on. Otherwise it trades
  reliability for speed.

- Run at **reduced input resolution** (e.g. downsampled to a few hundred
  px on the long side, upsampled afterward). Depth-map cost scales with
  resolution², and the region-growing step below only needs coarse,
  locally-consistent depth, not fine per-pixel detail.

## 2. Per-subject seeded region growing

The seeds are the people who stay visible. For each of them, a 
spatially-connected foreground mask is grown outward from the
face box (from Question 1's detector + tracker), constrained by depth
similarity.

- **Seed depth**: the median depth over the face box (robust to monocular
  depth noise near edges, unlike a single-point sample). The surrounding body
  is discovered by the growing process.
- **Growth rule**: standard seeded region growing / flood fill
  (`cv2.floodFill`, or a depth-difference threshold + connected-component
  labeling), where a neighboring pixel is added to the mask if its local
  depth difference from its already-included neighbor is below a small
  tolerance. This is deliberately a *local*, step-to-step test. The region can
  only grow through connected pixels, so an unrelated object at the same
  distance as the subject is not kept.
- **Limiting the growth**: a local test can creep along a floor or wall that
  recedes smoothly (for example where the feet touch the floor), because
  there is no depth jump to stop at. So the growth is limited in two ways: it
  only runs inside a window around the face box, sized for a person's body
  (several face heights tall), and it stays within a depth band around the
  seed depth.

Cost here is negligible. This is classical image processing on an
already-computed depth map, not a learned model, and it runs on a small
region around each subject rather than on the whole frame.

## 3. Soft edges

Because the depth map already carries continuous values, a soft edge comes
almost for free from the same depth map: near the grown region's boundary,
`alpha = smoothstep(local_depth_difference)` instead of a hard binary
cutoff so to give a feathered sharp/blur transition.

If finer boundary quality turns out to matter in practice, a matting head can
be added later as a boundary-only refinement step, without changing anything
else in this design. It would start from the grown mask instead of running on
the whole frame.

## 4. Multiple subjects

Each person who stays visible gets their own seed and their own
independently-grown mask (subjects at different distances from the camera can
both stay sharp). The final "stays sharp" region is the union of all
per-subject masks. Everything outside it is blurred.

## 5. Efficiency levers that hold regardless of camera motion

- **Downsampled depth resolution**: coarse depth is enough for
  region growing to work correctly.
- **Temporal propagation of the mask**, not just the depth map: instead of
  recomputing depth and re-growing from scratch every frame, warp the
  previous mask forward (optical flow, or a lightweight recurrent hidden
  state if a learned component is ever added per #3) and refresh only
  periodically or on a large tracked-box change. 

## Interaction with Question 1's identity filter

The depth mask and the identity filter (allow-list) work independently and
can be combined. A face is blurred unless the person is on the allow-list
(Question 1). Depth then decides the rest of the frame: around each person
who stays visible, the grown mask stays sharp, and everything else (other
people's bodies, non-face objects, the background) is blurred. This covers
what a face-only pipeline doesn't address on its own.

## Compositing

The final frame is composed like this:

- fully sharp inside the union of the foreground masks,
- fully blurred outside it,
- the depth-driven alpha in a narrow band at the edge, which gives a
  feathered transition instead of a hard, blocky cutoff.
