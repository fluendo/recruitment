# Q1.3 — Improving accuracy on dark skin and bald people

The face detection system shows poor accuracy on two specific demographic groups: dark-skinned individuals and bald people. The approach to improve this would be:

1. **Diagnosis first:** Before changing anything, measure the problem. Segment evaluation metrics by group (dark skin vs light skin, bald vs non-bald) instead of looking only at global accuracy. This tells us how much worse each group performs and whether the issue is one of recall (not detecting) or precision (detecting incorrectly).

2. **Most likely cause: dataset imbalance.** The most likely reason is that the training dataset has insufficient representation of dark skin and bald people. The model simply has not seen enough examples to learn these patterns reliably.

3. **Direct solution: more data.** Collect and add images with sufficient representation of dark-skinned and bald individuals, then retrain the model.

4. **Data augmentation as a complement:** If acquiring more data is difficult, augmentation can help — varying lighting, contrast, and brightness to simulate low-light conditions where dark skin is harder to detect. For bald people, augmentation is less effective since the problem is structural (absence of hair) rather than lighting-related.
