# Q1.6 — End-user experience improvements

There are two types of end users to consider: the client (the operator using Fluendo's anonymization tool) and the viewer (the person consuming the anonymized stream). Each requires a different approach.

**For the client (operator):**

1. **Configurability:** I would allow the operator to choose the anonymization type (blur, pixelation, masking) and its intensity, depending on the privacy level required by each use case.

2. **Selective mode:** Linked to the reidentification approach in [Q1.1](Q1_1.md), the operator could mark specific identities to preserve or anonymize before going live.

3. **Confidence indicators:** Visual feedback showing when the model is confident in its detections and when it is not, so the operator can be aware of potential blind spots.

4. **Live monitoring panel:** A separate panel alongside the stream output showing real-time metrics (detection rate, anonymization coverage, model confidence) and allowing the operator to adjust settings on the fly without interrupting the broadcast.

**For the viewer:**

5. **Low latency:** The anonymization processing must not add noticeable delay to the live stream. This is critical for real-time broadcasting.

6. **Temporal consistency:** The anonymization must be stable across frames. Flickering (a face appearing and disappearing anonymized between frames) is very distracting. This can be mitigated by applying temporal smoothing (bounding box interpolation) across consecutive frames.

7. **Natural appearance:** The anonymization should not be overly aggressive or distract from the content. By applying soft padding and feathered edges to the Gaussian blur, the anonymized regions blend smoothly into the scene, keeping it comprehensible and visually comfortable to watch.
