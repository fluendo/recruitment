# Q2.1 — Domain shift across different clubs and camera setups

The model performs well on validation data but fails on matches recorded in different clubs with different camera heights and angles. The most likely causes and how to investigate them:

**Root causes:**

1. **Domain shift:** The model was trained on data from specific clubs and has overfit to those camera conditions. When the angle or height changes, the perspective of the player's body looks different — the keypoints appear in relative positions that the model has not learned to recognize.

2. **Occlusions due to viewpoint:** A different camera angle can cause keypoints that were visible (e.g. wrist, elbow) to become occluded. If the model has not learned to handle these occlusions, it will fail.

3. **Environmental conditions:** Different lighting, court colors, and backgrounds change the input distribution compared to the training data.

**How to investigate systematically:**

4. **Collect failure samples:** Take videos from the clubs where the model fails and visualize the model's predictions. Identify where and how it fails — are the keypoints predicted incorrectly, or are the keypoints correct but the hit classification fails?

5. **Compare distributions:** Compare the characteristics of the training data versus the new data (camera angles, lighting, resolution). Detect what is different.

6. **Isolate the problem:** Determine whether the issue is in pose estimation (keypoints wrong) or in hit classification (keypoints correct but hit misclassified). This changes the solution.

7. **Possible fix:** Retrain or fine-tune with data from the new clubs. Alternatively, apply data augmentation that simulates different camera angles to make the model more robust from the start.
