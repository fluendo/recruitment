# Q2.2 — Inconsistent annotations and label quality

Different annotators labeled keypoints inconsistently (especially wrists and elbows), and hit timestamps are sometimes shifted by a few frames. The impact and how to address it:

**Impact on pose estimation:**

1. When annotators label keypoints differently, the model learns noisy targets. It will predict keypoints that are "in between" what different annotators would say — less precise predictions overall.

2. Wrists and elbows are critical for hit detection: the motion of the arm defines the type of hit. If these keypoints are inconsistent, everything that depends on them inherits the noise.

**Impact on hit classification:**

3. Shifted timestamps mean the model learns to associate hits with slightly wrong body positions. Since hits are brief events, a few frames shift can mean the difference between "before the hit" and "after the hit" motion.

4. Combined: the model has noisy input (keypoints) AND noisy labels (timestamps). Two sources of error that compound each other.

**How to detect label-quality problems at scale:**

5. **Loss sorting (model in the loop):** Train the model on the current data and compute the loss per sample. Sort samples by loss — the ones with the highest loss are the most suspicious, either mislabeled or very hard examples. Instead of reviewing the entire dataset, only inspect the high-loss tail, which is typically a small fraction of the total. This approach scales because it is automated: the model itself tells you where to look, without requiring double annotation.

6. **Statistical analysis of labels:** As a complement, detect outliers based on label distribution (e.g. timestamps clearly shifted beyond the expected range, keypoints outside the expected spatial distribution). Combining both signals — model-based (loss) and statistical (distribution) — gives a more complete picture of label quality.

**Decision criteria for relabel, filter, or reweight:**

7. **Relabel** when the problem is systematic and affects many samples (e.g. all annotators confuse the elbow position). Worth the investment because it improves quality permanently.

8. **Filter** when there are clearly wrong samples that cannot be easily fixed. Better to remove them than to leave noise in the dataset.

9. **Reweight** when samples are of intermediate quality but still usable — give them less weight in training so they do not affect the model as much, without losing data volume.
