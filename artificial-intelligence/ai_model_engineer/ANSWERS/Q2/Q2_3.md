# Q2.3 — Handling rare classes (smashes)

Smashes are rare in the dataset but very important for the product. Without changing the model architecture, the following changes can be considered:

**Dataset strategy:**

1. **Collect more smash data:** The most direct solution. If feasible, it is the best option — the model learns from real examples. Risk: smashes are not frequent in real matches, so collecting enough may be difficult.

2. **Oversampling:** Duplicate or increase the frequency of smash samples in training. Risk: if there are few distinct examples, the model may overfit to those specific cases.

3. **Data augmentation:** Apply transformations to the existing smash samples. An important caveat here: with keypoints, you cannot freely rotate or flip like with images, because keypoints have semantic meaning. Augmentations must respect the skeleton structure.

**Training strategy:**

4. **Class weighting:** Penalize errors on smashes more during training — give higher weight to that class in the loss. Tradeoff: it helps balance the learning, but if the weight is too high it can destabilize training and degrade performance on common hits.

**Evaluation strategy:**

5. **Do not use global accuracy:** With imbalanced classes, accuracy is misleading — if smashes are 5% of the data and the model misses all of them, accuracy is still 95%. Class-level metrics are necessary.

6. **Per-class recall and F1-score:** Specifically monitor the recall for smashes (how many are detected) and the F1 per class. A confusion matrix shows what smashes are being confused with.

**How to verify that improvements are real and not overfitting artifacts:**

7. **Held-out test set** with enough smash examples that the model has never seen during training.

8. **Check that common hits do not degrade:** The improvement in smashes should not come at the cost of common hits. Compare per-class metrics before and after to confirm the tradeoff is acceptable.
