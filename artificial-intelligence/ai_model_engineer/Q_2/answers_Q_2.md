1. The model shows strong performance on validation data but fails on new matches recorded in different clubs with different camera heights and angles. Hit detection accuracy drops significantly.
<br>Explain the most likely root causes of this failure and describe how you would systematically investigate and isolate the problem.

> The likely cause is distribution shift. The conditions of the training data changed, thus the model no longer performs as expected. To investigate the problem: installing cameras at different angles in the same club, we can use one camera for training and validation and another at a different angle for testing. If the accuracy drops in test, we can confirm that changing the angle of the recording is the cause. Next we can build a dataset mixing cameras at different angles. We expect the model trained with this dataset to have good accuracy with cameras placed at angles covered in the dataset and in between. Another option is that other conditions change aside from camera angle: lighting, player clothing, court color, image compression, etc.  If the first test on angle sensitivity fails, I would study those other factors. Finally check data leackage too.

2. After reviewing some samples, you discover that different annotators labeled keypoints slightly differently (especially wrists and elbows), and hit timestamps are sometimes shifted by a few frames.
<br>Analyze how inconsistent annotations affect both pose estimation and hit classification, how you would detect label-quality problems at scale and how you would decide whether to relabel, filter, or reweigh parts of the dataset. Justify your decision criteria.

> We have label bias by some annotators. To systematically analyze label bias, since the keypont positions are continuous valued vectors, I would use statistical tests such as t-tests between anotators. And for the case of timestamps, compare distribution errors. Then see if the model presents high loss for anotators with statistically significant differences. Both errors (keypoints and timestamps) add noise to the training data. This reduces accuracy in the case of pose estimation (keypoints), and also in the classifier used for hit tipology (inconsistent pose prediction and timestamps). Depending on the results of the first and second tests i would decide whether to relabel, filter or reweight. To make this decision it is essential to know data abundancy and labeling cost. For instance, if we have a systematic annotation difference, high loss, abundant data and high annotation cost, we will filter. If data is scarce and annotation cheap, relabel. If scarce data and expensive annotation we may want to reweight to reduce impact of noisy annotations without reducing dataset size. 

3. Smashes are rare in the dataset but very important for the product. The model often misses them while performing well on common hits.
<br>Without changing the model architecture, explain which dataset, training, and evaluation strategy changes you would consider. Here, the idea is to discuss trade-offs and risks of each approach and how you would verify that improvements are real and not overfitting artifacts.

> Dataset: Resampling to increase smashes proportion. Inconvenients: reducing proportion of common hits, may hurt overall accuracy. If getting extra samples is too expensive we can do synthetic augmentation of the existing ones. Training: Using stratified k-fold cross-validation. The number k can be determined according to the number of smashes (or smashes per group of data) to assure we have enough samples of smashes in each test set. Evaluation: do not use accuracy, use recall, precision or F1 metric instead, or even a more informative output: confusion matrix for the hit classification. Also evaluate hit timing error.

4. Two training runs with the same configuration produce noticeably different results. One model is stable but less accurate; another is more accurate but produces jittery keypoints and inconsistent hit timing.
<br>Provide a reasoning to decide which model is actually better for production. What additional tests, metrics, and scenario-based evaluations would you design to support the decision?

> One hypothesis is that higher accuracy with jittery keypoints and inconsistent hit timing are due to overfitting + data leackage (model learns data noise). In that case I expect the stable model to generalize better to unseen data. I would first charachterize the keypoint variability and hit timing of both models and test them in new augmented data with some added noise (light, blur, distorsion, etc.) or unseen data from a different club or camera location and measure what is the keypoint variability and hit timing and accuracy degradation. If accuracy drops more in these new tests in the model that gave best accuracy first, that would confirm overfitting. This is not surprising since performing several trainings and picking the one with best accuracy is a type of data leackage (cheating).
